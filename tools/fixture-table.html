Automation did not arrive in the warehouse all at once. The company first introduced handheld scanners, then conveyor sorting, then robots that could lift a pallet without being told where to put it. Each step was announced as relief from drudgery, and each step did remove some. What it also did — more quietly — was reassign the judgement to the machine and the remaining worker to the machine's errors.

Globalisation pulled in the other direction. Cheap freight meant the company could move a distribution centre overseas, and it did, then brought it back when the freight stopped being cheap. Through both moves, the people who had learned the scanners and the conveyor and the robots kept their jobs, because they were the only ones who could tell when the system was wrong.
