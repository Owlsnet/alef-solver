Automation did not arrive in the warehouse all at once. The company first introduced handheld scanners, then conveyor sorting, then robots that could lift a pallet without being told where to put it. Each step was announced as relief from drudgery, and each step did remove some — while quietly moving the judgement to the machine and the remaining worker to the machine's errors.

Globalisation pulled the other way. Cheap freight let the company move a distribution centre overseas, and it did, then brought it back when freight stopped being cheap. Through both moves, the people who had learned the scanners and the conveyor and the robots kept their jobs, because they were the only ones who could tell when the system was wrong.
